01 / School meals

A food program worth celebrating

Montair is part of San Ramon Valley Unified School District’s school meal program.

Eat Real certification

One of three Gold-certified districts

SRVUSD is one of three districts nationwide listed at Eat Real’s Gold tier, as checked in October 2026. The certification evaluates a district’s food program across nutrition, sourcing, sustainability and education.

See Eat Real’s certified districts ↗
Fresh choices

Fresh produce, every day

SRVUSD offers a daily Harvest Bar with fresh fruits and vegetables at all schools. Its 2026–27 elementary-school update says all produce served is 100% certified organic.

Read the district’s meal update ↗
Program nutrition

Less added sugar

Eat Real’s August 2026 profile reports added sugars below 7% of meal calories in SRVUSD’s program, alongside more scratch cooking and minimally processed ingredients.

Read Eat Real’s district profile ↗

Certification and reported figures describe the district program; individual menu items vary. SRVUSD confirms breakfast and lunch are available at no charge to students for 2026–27. Thank you to the cafeteria and nutrition teams who make these meals possible.

02 / Our wellness policy

Wellness throughout the school day

Board Policy 5030 connects nutrition, physical activity, learning and family participation.

Celebrations & fundraising

Healthy food and non-food options

The policy directs district leadership to encourage healthy-food or non-food fundraisers and encourage staff to avoid non-nutritious food rewards. Staff are to encourage families to consider nutrition when donating party snacks; celebrations should follow lunch when possible.

Read the policy’s food guidance ↗
Learning & daily routines

Education, movement and water

The policy calls for nutrition education, regular opportunities for physical activity and free drinking water during meals. It also directs district leadership to encourage staff to model healthy eating and physical activity.

Explore the wellness policy ↗
Community participation

A role for families and staff

Parents, students and community members are encouraged to participate in policy review. The superintendent must designate district or school employees to ensure each school follows the policy.

03 / Nutrition guidance

A little help reading the label

Published guidance for families and volunteers choosing foods for children.

American Heart Association

Added sugar: a daily guide

The AHA’s child-specific guidance recommends no more than 25 grams of added sugar a day for ages 2–18, across all foods and drinks. This is a daily recommendation, not a per-snack allowance.

Read the AHA’s guidance ↗
U.S. Food & Drug Administration

Added sugars and total sugars

“Total Sugars” includes naturally occurring sugars, such as those in fruit and milk, plus any added sugar. The “Added Sugars” line shows the added amount per serving. Check the serving size when comparing products.

Learn how to read added sugars ↗
How USDA Smart Snacks relates to school snacks

Smart Snacks sets standards for foods sold separately to students on campus during the school day, outside the school meal programs. Qualifying foods include whole-grain-rich products, foods with fruit, vegetables, dairy or a protein food as the first ingredient, and certain fruit or vegetable combinations. Other nutrient limits also apply.

The standard’s snack limit is 200 calories per item or package sold, with exemptions for certain foods. It is not a universal portion recommendation. Donated classroom snacks and celebrations are addressed by local wellness policies.

USDA Smart Snacks guide ↗ · California’s school-food guidance ↗

California’s 2027 food-dye timeline

California’s statewide prohibition on Red 3 in food products begins January 1, 2027. Separate restrictions on Blue 1, Blue 2, Green 3, Red 40, Yellow 5 and Yellow 6 in competitive school foods begin December 31, 2027. Competitive foods are foods sold separately from school meals.
